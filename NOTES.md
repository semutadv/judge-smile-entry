# How the line was built

The brief asks for one line, at most 200 characters, about a small good
thing that happened today — judged on subject first, then warmth, then
surprise, then one concrete detail, then fresh wording. The line:

> The warung coffee I ordered came with an extra fried banana, still warm, on the house.

Clause by clause:

- **"The warung coffee I ordered"** — the subject is deliberately ordinary.
  Not a café latte with a name, just the coffee a person actually ordered
  today at a warung. Smallness is the point of the brief.
- **"came with an extra fried banana"** — the good thing arrives unasked.
  "Extra" does the surprise work: it was not ordered, not expected, and
  not on the bill. A fried banana (pisang goreng) is also a specific,
  regional, slightly unexpected object next to a coffee.
- **"still warm"** — the one concrete detail. Warmth here is literal
  before it is emotional: the banana had just left the fryer, which means
  somebody fried it moments before deciding to give it away.
- **"on the house"** — the closing turn. Three short words convert a
  snack into a kindness: it was a gift, and the sentence ends on the
  giver's generosity rather than the receiver's luck.

What was cut: an earlier draft named the warung and the hour, but the
extra clauses crowded out the ending. The final line keeps exactly one
detail (the warmth) and lets "on the house" land as the last beat.

Character count: 86 of the allowed 200 — short on purpose, since length
for its own sake does not count with this judge.
